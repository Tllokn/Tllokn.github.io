Luzhe Sun 孙鲁喆

Ph.D. Candidate, TTIC · Robotics Planning and Diffusion Models

I'm a Ph.D. candidate at TTIC advised by Professor Matthew R. Walter in the Robot Intelligence through Perception Lab (RIPL). Before TTIC, I completed my master's in Computer Science at the University of Chicago and my undergraduate studies at Xiamen University.

Research interests: My research focuses on robot learning and human–robot collaboration, with the goal of building robots that can adapt to unfamiliar situations and work reliably alongside people. I develop generative methods for shared autonomy and policy adaptation, using diffusion, consistency, and flow models to refine robot actions while preserving human intent or task goals. I also study visual grounding and hallucination in vision-language models, with an emphasis on understanding and detecting failures in multimodal perception.

I also enjoy photography, Chinese painting, and playing the Erhu.

Publications

Selected Publications

Flow-based Policy Adaptation without Policy Updates

Luzhe Sun*, Jingtian Ji*, Haoran Chen, Jiawei Zhou, Matthew R. Walter
Website arXiv

TL;DR: GLOVES uses flow matching to selectively pull out-of-distribution actions from humans or pretrained policies toward expert behavior—without updating the underlying policy. With limited demonstrations, it improves imperfect imitation-learning and VLA agents across simulated and real-robot tasks.

SABRE: Scalable and Automated Benchmarking of VLMs under Stress

Zixuan Lan*, Luzhe Sun*, Matthew R. Walter, Jiawei Zhou
Website Code arXiv

TL;DR: SABRE turns natural-language test specifications into automatically generated, filtered, and human-verified VLM stress tests that can evolve with the models. Its SABRE-Prior benchmark exposes heavy reliance on world priors: six frontier VLMs score only 17.8–31.3% macro accuracy.

What Are We Actually Benchmarking in Robot Manipulation?

Tianchong Jiang, Xiangshan Tan*, Samuel Wheeler*, Luzhe Sun*, Tewodros W. Ayalew*, and Matthew Walter
CoRL 2026 [Oral] Best Abstract · IROS 2026 RGMCW Website Code arXiv

TL;DR: Auditing five popular robot-manipulation benchmarks with four diagnostics reveals shortcuts, statistically unresolved gains, overfitting, and training-data proximity effects—especially on LIBERO, CALVIN, and SimplerEnv. The results show why benchmark scores alone often do not establish general manipulation progress.

Seeing without Looking: Do Vision-Language Benchmarks Really Test Vision?

Zixuan Lan*, Luzhe Sun*, Matthew R. Walter, Jiawei Zhou
CVPR 2026 GRAIL-V Workshop Website Code arXiv

TL;DR: Across seven open VLMs, benchmark accuracy often remains surprisingly high after fine-grained visual evidence is masked, corrupted, or replaced: removing 75% of image tokens reduces POPE accuracy by only about three percentage points for some models. These results reveal a gap between benchmark performance and genuine visual grounding, motivating evaluations that measure whether predictions respond appropriately to changes in visual evidence.

FlashBack: Consistency Model-Accelerated Shared Autonomy

Luzhe Sun, Jingtian Ji, Xiangshan(Vincent) Tan, Matthew R. Walter
CoRL 2025 [Spotlight] Website Code arXiv Blog(知乎) Livestream

TL;DR: FlashBack distills diffusion into a one-step consistency-model copilot that corrects user actions in real time without knowing their goal, reward, or policy. It outperforms iterative diffusion assistance on demanding control tasks and raises real-robot peg-insertion success from 66.7% to 83.3%.

StackGen: Generating Stable Structures from Silhouettes via Diffusion

Luzhe Sun*, Takuma Yoneda*, Samuel W. Wheeler, Tianchong Jiang, Matthew R. Walter
IROS 2025 [Oral] Website arXiv Blog(知乎)

TL;DR: StackGen learns to generate diverse 6-DoF block arrangements that are both stable and faithful to a user-provided silhouette, avoiding explicit physics models or combinatorial search. It achieves 85.2% simulated stability and successfully builds all eight tested structures with a UR5 robot.

To the Noise and Back: Diffusion for Shared Autonomy

Takuma Yoneda, Luzhe Sun, Ge Yang, Bradly Stadie, Matthew R. Walter
RSS 2023 [Oral] Website Code arXiv

TL;DR: This work introduces partial diffusion for shared autonomy: noise and denoise a user's command toward demonstrated expert behavior, with a single knob trading off intent preservation and assistance. It needs no reward, dynamics, predefined goals, or user-policy data during training and improves both simulated and real-robot teleoperation.

More Publications

Detecting Hallucinations in Vision-Language Models without Generating a Single Token

Sai Akhil Kogilathota, Sripadha Vallabha E G, Luzhe Sun, Jiawei Zhou
EACL 2026 Main [Oral] Code arXiv

TL;DR: HALP predicts whether a VLM will hallucinate before decoding by probing its visual and multimodal hidden states in one forward pass. Across eight VLMs, lightweight probes reach up to 0.93 AUROC while adding less than 1% overhead relative to full generation, enabling early abstention or selective routing.

Decompositional Quantum Graph Neural Network

Xing Ai, Luzhe Sun, Zhihong Zhang, Junchi Yan, Edwin Hancock
arXiv

TL;DR: egoQGNN decomposes arbitrarily large graphs into ego-graphs that fit a fixed-size quantum device and uses a trainable Euclidean-to-Hilbert mapping to limit information loss. In simulation, it achieves competitive classification accuracy on six molecular and protein datasets with just 43 parameters—1.68% as many as the smallest classical GNN baseline.
